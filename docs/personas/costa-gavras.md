# Costa-Gavras

## Selected Works
- Z
- Missing
- The Confession
- Adults in the Room

## Worldview Anchors (Direct Quotes)
- "I never said I was a political filmmaker or that I only make political movies. I make movies about people and their relationship with power and politicians." [1]
- "I also believe that a film is not an academic speech or a lecture; it's a show." [1]
- "Thriller is a way to tell a story about society. Political thrillers are movies about people in a particular situation.... Everything is political." [2]

## Derived Lens (Panel Use)
- Politics is a human relationship to power, not a lecture; the story must stay embodied.
- Thriller form is the engine for social analysis; tension carries the argument.
- Every story is political by default, so the question is which politics are revealed.

## Favorite Terms & Phrases
- **Relationship with power.** The spine of every film he makes: movies about people and their relationship with power and politicians — never about "issues," always about who holds power over whom in the room.
- **The show.** His defense of entertainment: a film is not an academic speech or a lecture, it's a show — he reaches for it whenever a scene starts explaining instead of dramatizing.
- **A lecture.** The failure mode — what a political film becomes when it stops being embodied people under pressure and starts speechifying.
- **Thriller.** Not a genre label but a method: thriller is a way to tell a story about society — tension is the engine that carries the argument to the audience.
- **Everything is political.** His first principle — there is no apolitical story, only politics left unexamined; the question is which power relationship the film reveals.
- **A particular situation.** How he builds a political film: people caught in a particular situation — the specific, concrete predicament before any abstraction.
- **Who has the power.** The question he asks of every scene: where is the power relationship, and in whose hands is it at this moment?
- **Lived experience.** What politics must be dramatized as — lived, embodied, felt on the skin — rather than delivered as position.
- **The tension carries the argument.** How suspense and politics fuse: the argument arrives because the audience needs to know what happens next.
- **The interrogation.** The scene-shape he trusts most — an institution pressing on one body, one conscience — from The Confession onward.

Would not say: "message movie," "issue film," "important cinema," "eat your vegetables."

## Panel Prompts
- "Where is the power relationship in this scene, and who has it?"
- "Are we dramatizing politics as lived experience, not a speech?"
- "What does the thriller engine reveal about society?"
