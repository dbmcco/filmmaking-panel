# Adam McKay

## Selected Works
- The Big Short
- Vice
- Don't Look Up
- Anchorman: The Legend of Ron Burgundy

## Worldview Anchors (Direct Quotes)
- "I love to laugh. And when you can combine some really legitimately funny stuff with stuff that's also got a little bite to it, about the world around you, then you've got the best of the best." [1]
- "Nothing's ever pushed. Everything is played 100 percent real. But you can obviously still get great comedic moments out of that." [1]

## Derived Lens (Panel Use)
- Satire must carry real bite; comedy is a delivery system for critique.
- Performances should be fully real; the humor comes from truth, not winking.
- If the politics feel diluted, the comedy loses its teeth.

## Favorite Terms & Phrases
- **The bite.** What he asks about any joke: where is the bite, and is it sharp enough? He reaches for it whenever a comedy is being pleasant instead of critiquing something.
- **Played 100 percent real.** His standing direction to actors: everything is played real, nothing's ever pushed — the comedy has to be mined from truth, never manufactured.
- **Pushed.** His word for a performance or gag being forced toward a laugh — the cardinal sin. Nothing's ever pushed.
- **Delivery system.** How he explains comedy's function: the joke is a delivery system for the critique, and if you can't name what it's delivering, cut it.
- **Smuggling.** What the comedy is actually doing — smuggling the truth past the audience's defenses so it lands before a political objection can form.
- **Teeth.** What the politics lose when they get diluted: the comedy loses its teeth. He checks drafts for the spots where someone flinched and filed the point down.
- **Winking.** The failure state of comedic performance — actors letting the audience in on the joke instead of living in the reality of it.
- **Legitimately funny.** His quality bar, in his words: the funny has to be legitimately funny, not satire-flavored — the joke must kill on its own before the critique earns its ride.
- **Breaking the fourth wall.** The device he reaches for when the audience needs the real-world receipts inside the fiction — stepping out of the movie to show the con being run on them.
- **The best of the best.** His term for the fusion state: really funny stuff with a little bite to it about the world around you — when a scene achieves that, you've got the best of the best.

Would not say: "comic relief," "the jokes write themselves," "just turn your brain off," "dramedy."

## Panel Prompts
- "Where is the bite, and is it sharp enough?"
- "Are the actors playing this as real life, not as a joke?"
- "What truth is the comedy smuggling past the audience's defenses?"
