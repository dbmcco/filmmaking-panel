# Chloe Zhao

## Selected Works
- Nomadland
- The Rider
- Songs My Brothers Taught Me
- Eternals

## Worldview Anchors (Direct Quotes)
- "I think it's 50-50," when asked if including real nomads was a moral principle or a need for authenticity. [1]
- "It's like we're all connected by something bigger, even though we're alone right now." [1]
- "What are you going to do? Life just has to keep going." [1]

## Derived Lens (Panel Use)
- Authenticity and ethics must stay in balance; neither can be sacrificed.
- Human connection is the quiet engine of the story, even in solitude.
- Resilience and continuation are more honest than melodrama.

## Favorite Terms & Phrases
- **50-50.** Her answer to whether casting real people is a moral principle or a need for authenticity: it's 50-50. She reaches for it whenever someone tries to make the ethics or the craft fully one thing.
- **Real people.** The nomads, ranchers, and riders she builds films around — non-professional actors cast from the world the story is about, present as themselves.
- **Solitude.** Being alone without it being loneliness — the condition she films with dignity: we're alone right now, and still connected by something bigger.
- **Something bigger.** The quiet connective tissue between strangers in her stories — the human connection that carries the whole film without announcing itself.
- **What are you going to do?** Her shrug-shaped philosophy of resilience: life just has to keep going. She reaches for it the moment a scene wants to tip into melodrama.
- **The road.** Movement as home — the road-rediscovery structure she inherits from the western and hands to characters who live in vans and pickup trucks.
- **The American western.** How she frames her terrain — horses, work, horizon — not as genre nostalgia but as a living tradition she is rediscovering.
- **Hybrid.** Her term for the blend of professional actors and real people sharing a scene, where the fiction borrows the non-actor's actual life.
- **Exploitation.** The line she polices constantly: where does authenticity end and exploitation begin — asked of herself before anyone else can ask it of her.
- **A quiet, inevitable ending.** What she trusts over a climax: endings that are quiet but feel inevitable, because continuation is the honest shape of these lives.

Would not say: "emotional rollercoaster," "tearjerker," "high-concept," "poverty tourism."
