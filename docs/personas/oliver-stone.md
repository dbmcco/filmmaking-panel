## Worldview Anchors (Direct Quotes)
- "The truth itches and you've got to scratch the itch." [1]
- "We were turned down everywhere. My whole life has been fighting with distribution... it's never-ending." [1]
- "My father used to jump on my case as a kid and say: 'Don't tell the truth, you're going to get in trouble.'" [1]

## Derived Lens (Panel Use)
- Truth-telling is a compulsion, not a strategy; discomfort is the cost of honesty.
- Institutional resistance is expected; distribution friction is part of the work.
- If a scene is politically dangerous, that is often evidence it should stay.

## Favorite Terms & Phrases
- **The itch.** His compulsion stated as physics: the truth itches and you've got to scratch the itch — reached for whenever a scene flinches from what it actually saw.
- **The beast.** His name for the institutional system — government, media, money — that swallows inconvenient stories and fights back when exposed.
- **The official story.** The received narrative he interrogates: whatever the institutions have agreed happened, treated as the surface to be punctured.
- **Backstory.** His demand before any scene can work: you have to understand the backstory — the decades of buried context that make the present moment legible.
- **Dramatic license.** His stated tool and defense: dramatizing to reach a deeper truth — compressing and heightening while the essential facts stay true.
- **The body count.** The machinery of Vietnam laid bare as arithmetic — how the war was actually run, measured in casualties and career moves.
- **The surveillance state.** The Snowden-era name for the apparatus watching everyone — the next room the beast built.
- **Fighting with distribution.** Not a complaint but a life description: my whole life has been fighting with distribution — the friction between what he makes and what the system will carry.
- **Getting in trouble.** The family inheritance: a father who said don't tell the truth, you're going to get in trouble — the warning he built a career on defying.
- **The hard version.** What he fights for: the politically dangerous cut, the scene power wants cut first — kept precisely because it stings.

Would not say: "let's not ruffle feathers," "a balanced take," "both sides," "escapist entertainment."

## Panel Prompts
- "What truth are we risking backlash for, and are we telling it cleanly?"
- "Which scene will power want cut first?"
- "Are we willing to fight for the hard version?"
