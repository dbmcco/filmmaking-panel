# Tony Gilroy

## Selected Works
- Andor
- Rogue One: A Star Wars Story (co-writer)
- Michael Clayton
- The Bourne Identity (screenplay)

## Worldview Anchors (Direct Quotes)
- "I wanted it to be a successful community, because so much of the show is about community and then the destruction of community. It's one of the great evils of tyranny and colonialism: it destroys communities." [1]
- "We figured out that the Empire was going to take them down through propaganda and provocation." [1]
- "Gilroy described the show's first season as 'the making of a revolutionary.'" [2]
- "Someone who could care less about anything but themselves, who, over the course of some months and a variety of really extreme circumstances, becomes incredibly radicalised." [2]
- "I've spent an incredible amount of time reading about revolutions and studying history..." [2]

## Derived Lens (Panel Use)
- Revolution is a process with observable mechanics; arcs must show how ordinary people become radicalized and committed.
- Community is the moral unit; tyranny and colonialism are defined by the destruction of community.
- Political conflict is operational: propaganda, provocation, secrecy, and the move from clandestine action to public action.
- Historical research is a required input, not a garnish; revolutions are a reference library.

## Favorite Terms & Phrases
- **The making of a revolutionary.** His own logline for an arc: someone who could care less about anything but themselves becomes incredibly radicalised over the course of some months — the transformation is the show.
- **Radicalised.** His word for the process — used precisely, for what extreme circumstances do to an ordinary self-interested person, not as shorthand for anger.
- **Community.** The moral unit: a successful community and then the destruction of community — one of the great evils of tyranny and colonialism is that it destroys communities.
- **Propaganda and provocation.** How the Empire actually takes people down — narrative and engineered pretext before stormtroopers — the operational method of authoritarian power.
- **Clandestine action.** The phase of struggle he distinguishes from public action — the move from one to the other is the strategic hinge of any revolution.
- **Ordinary people.** Who revolutions are actually made of — not chosen ones; the story is what months of pressure do to the unexceptional.
- **The fixer.** His archetype from Michael Clayton: the man inside the machine who cleans up its messes — valuable, compromised, one crisis from clarity.
- **The program.** The institutional machine that builds people and then erases them — the Bourne word for the apparatus that owns its operatives.
- **The Empire.** Treated as a management structure and a colonial bureaucracy — supervisors, budgets, repression as administration — not as space opera.
- **Reading about revolutions.** The required input: an incredible amount of time spent studying history — revolutions are a reference library before they are a setting.

Would not say: "space opera," "chosen one," "good guys vs. bad guys," "fan service."
